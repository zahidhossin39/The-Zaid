### 2. Problem List
**Category: Pacing & Density**
* **Severity:** Critical
* **Timestamp:** 0-13.6s
* **Why it hurts understanding:** You compressed a story that requires reading into 13.6 seconds. The velocity completely exceeds human reading speed. By the time a user realizes a call is happening, the video is already refilling a fitness class. It looks like a chaotic dashboard glitching out rather than a coherent narrative.

**Category: Story Structure**
* **Severity:** Critical
* **Timestamp:** 0-0.8s
* **Why it hurts understanding:** Flashing the final poster for 0.8 seconds at the very beginning is a massive unforced error. It spoils the ending, vanishes before it can be read, and disorients the viewer right when they land on the page. 

**Category: Motion Craft**
* **Severity:** Major
* **Timestamp:** 7.0-7.6s
* **Why it hurts understanding:** The vertical slide wipe between Dental and Fitness feels like a PowerPoint transition. It breaks the spatial reality of the scene. These are supposed to be two things happening in the same afternoon, but the wipe makes it feel like two disconnected slides in a deck.

**Category: Design**
* **Severity:** Minor
* **Element:** Fitness card seat dots (7.8-10.3s)
* **Why it hurts understanding:** You used 12 graphical dots to represent seats. They occupy premium vertical space and add visual clutter to a card that is already overloaded with text messages and chips. It adds cognitive load for zero narrative gain.

**Category: Story Tension**
* **Severity:** Minor
* **Timestamp:** 1.8-2.2s
* **Why it hurts understanding:** The "Voicemail in 3" arc is a great concept, but it drops from 3 to 1 in less than a second. The viewer has no time to feel the anxiety of a missed customer before the AI intercepts it.

### 3. Concrete Fixes
1. **Pacing:** Extend the total loop to 22 seconds. Give the human brain time to read the text.
2. **The First 3 Seconds:** Delete the 0.8s opening poster flash entirely. Open directly on the Dental card already in crisis (ringing). Let the poster serve *only* as the final 4-second resting state.
3. **Motion:** Replace the vertical slide wipe with a continuous horizontal camera pan. Treat the two businesses as existing side by side on a shared physical surface.
4. **Design:** Kill the 12 seat dots on the Fitness card. Use purely numeric capacity (e.g., "11/12") to strip out clutter and give the text room to breathe.
5. **Tension:** Slow down the voicemail countdown. Give it 2.5 seconds to build actual stakes before the AI answers.

### 4. Redesigned Beat Sheet (Two-Business Version)

**Format & Site Integration:**
* **Website Slot:** The 440x550 desktop (327x409 mobile) slot shown in the v4 frames is excellent. A 4:5 vertical ratio maximizes visual impact in a 1/3 column without squashing the adjacent headline. Do not revert to the outdated 16:9 (316x176) slot.
* **Canvas:** 1080x1350 (4:5 aspect ratio).
* **Minimum Font Size:** 60px on canvas (renders at an extremely safe 18px on a 327px mobile display).

**Beats & Timing (22s Total Length):**
* **0-4s: The Threat (Dental).** Open in crisis. Brightside Dental is "With a patient". A call is ringing. The arc ticks "Voicemail in 3... 2... 1" deliberately over 2.5 seconds.
* **4-9s: The Catch.** The arc vanishes. "Answered for you" pill pops. The live transcript types at human reading speed: "Toothache. Today?" -> "Can you do 4:30?" -> "Perfect, see you then." Big amber pill: "Booked 4:30 PM". Hold for 1.5s to let it sink in.
* **9-10.5s: The Pan.** The entire frame (except the bottom tally) pans smoothly to the left. Ember Fitness slides into the center. State: "Teaching a class".
* **10.5-16s: The Leak and Refill.** Maya texts "Can't make it, sorry!". The capacity text drops to 11/12. A chip pops: "Texted 3 on waitlist". A reply arrives: "I'll take it!". Capacity updates to 12/12 Full (highlighted in amber). Hold for 1.5s.
* **16-18s: The Relief.** The "Class done. Nothing to chase." card drops in, dimming the chat thread behind it.
* **18-22s: The Poster.** The "While you were busy" poster slides up to cover everything. It holds completely still for 4 full seconds so the user can read the summary. Seamless fade back to 0s.

### 5. Prediction & Riskiest Assumption
* **Predicted Score:** 9.2/10
* **Riskiest Assumption:** Assuming hero visitors will watch a video for 22 seconds. Hero sections are highly transient. If a user starts scrolling after 5 seconds, they will only see the Dental resolution and miss the Fitness refill entirely. The bet is that seeing even *one* fully resolved booking in the first 9 seconds is enough to prove the value and earn the CTA click.
