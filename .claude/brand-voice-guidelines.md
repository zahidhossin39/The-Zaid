# Brand voice guidelines: Zaid Hossain

Writing only. For anyone (human or AI) writing as Zaid: website copy first, then emails, proposals and posts.

Sources: the portfolio site copy (hero, problem, what I build, proof, process, about, FAQ, book a call) and Zaid's own answers (September 2026).

---

## 1. Who is speaking

Zaid is one person who builds **websites and automations** for small businesses, both local shops and online businesses, anywhere in the world. He is the brand. There is no "we", no team, no agency voice.

The reader is a busy owner who is not technical, knows exactly what they want, and has been putting it off.

**One-line voice:** a calm, honest builder who explains things simply and does what he says.

*Confidence: High (site copy + direct answers agree).*

---

## 2. We are / we are not

| Zaid is | Zaid is not |
|---|---|
| **Honest.** Says what he can and can't do. "If I'm not the right fit, I'll say so on the call." | Salesy. No hype, no pressure, no "limited spots". |
| **Simple.** Plain words an owner understands on the first read. | Techy. No API, webhook, CRM, stack, pipeline unless the reader used the word first. |
| **Calm and professional.** Warm, steady, confident. | Casual or slangy. No jokes, memes, "super", "awesome", exclamation marks. |
| **Reliable.** Promises small, specific things and keeps them. | Overpromising. No "10x", "guaranteed growth", "skyrocket". |
| **Fast.** Short sentences, quick to the point, quick to reply. | Rushed or vague. Speed never replaces a clear answer. |
| **Personal.** "I", "you", small real details about how he works. | Corporate. No "solutions", "leverage", "synergy", "empower". |

*Confidence: High.*

---

## 3. Voice rules (always the same)

1. **Write "I" and "you".** Never "we" or "our team". Zaid works alone and says so.
2. **Start with their problem, then the fix.** Most sentences are about the client's situation. Zaid appears as the person who fixes it, not the subject of the page.
3. **One idea per sentence.** Aim for under 20 words. Break long sentences.
4. **Plain words.** Say "set up", not "implement". "Use", not "leverage". "Website", not "web presence".
5. **Concrete over abstract.** Name the real thing: "the report you rebuild every Monday", "enquiries that sit in an inbox for a day". Not "manual workflows".
6. **Promise only what is true.** Use real facts from how he works: fixed price agreed upfront, the final payment only when it works, you own the code and accounts, he replies himself. Never invent numbers or results.
7. **No humor.** Stay calm and straight. Dry wit is out.
8. **No exclamation marks.** Confidence comes from facts, not punctuation.
9. **Neutral English.** Readable anywhere. Avoid idioms and local references that don't travel.

*Confidence: High for 1-6 (site copy + answers). Medium for 7-9 (from answers only).*

---

## 4. Tone by context (flexes)

The voice stays fixed. Only formality and length change.

| Context | Formality | Length | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website headlines | Medium | Very short | "You've had the idea long enough." |
| Website body | Medium | Short paragraphs | "You explain what's stuck in plain words. I handle the technical side." |
| Buttons and labels | Low | 2-5 words | "Book a free 20-min call" |
| Cold email / DM | Medium | 4-6 sentences | "I noticed your booking page only takes phone calls. I build simple online booking that drops straight into your calendar. Worth a 20-minute call?" |
| Proposal / client email | Higher | As needed, well structured | "Here's what I'll build, the fixed price, and the date you'll have it." |

*Confidence: Medium (website rows from copy; email rows inferred from answers).*

---

## 5. Words

**Use:** website, automation, set up, build, fixed price, handed over working, you own it, stuck, booking, enquiry, customers, hours back, plain words, small working pieces.

**Avoid → say instead**

| Avoid | Say |
|---|---|
| solution, offering | website, automation (name the thing) |
| leverage, utilize | use |
| implement, deploy | set up, build, launch |
| workflow, pipeline | process, the steps |
| CRM, API, webhook | your customer list, connects to, sends to (unless the client uses the term) |
| seamless, cutting-edge, robust | say what it does |
| we, our team | I |
| grow your business 10x | the real, specific result |

*Confidence: Medium.*

## 6. Examples from the site

**On voice (keep):**
- "Knowing what to build was never the problem."
- "Twenty minutes, no pitch. If I'm not the right fit for it, I'll say so on the call."
- "You keep the code and every account."
- "A fixed price, agreed upfront, before any work starts."

**Off voice (fix):**
- "Built with Astro" in the footer is a technical detail the owner doesn't need. Cut it or change it to "Built by me."
- The em dashes used through the site ("built small — built to get used") make sentences run on. Use periods.

*Confidence: High.*

---

## 7. Quick check before publishing

- [ ] Could a non-technical owner understand every sentence on the first read?
- [ ] Is it about their problem more than about me?
- [ ] Any "we", jargon, hype, jokes or exclamation marks? Remove them.
- [ ] Is every promise something I actually do?
- [ ] Is every sentence under about 20 words?

## Open questions

### High priority
1. **Existing lines with a dry edge.** The site has "Same idea. The date keeps sliding." and "You've had the idea long enough." These are a little wry, and you chose no humor.
   - Recommendation: keep both. They are plain statements of the reader's situation, not jokes. Drop any line that exists only to be clever.
   - Need from you: confirm or override.

### Medium priority
2. **Em dashes.** They appear in many site lines.
   - Recommendation: replace them with periods or commas during the copy pass. It makes the text calmer and easier to read.
   - Need from you: confirm.
3. **Real details about how you work.** "Very personal" works best with true specifics.
   - Recommendation: add 2-3 facts you're happy to share (where you're based, time zone, how fast you reply, what you built for yourself).
   - Need from you: the facts.
