# Rescore — Kimi, revised prototypes 07–10

Scored against SCORING.md and LAB-SPEC.md, on the files as they currently exist on disk (not as intended). Bar: both scores ≥ 8.5.

## 07 · Aubergine Hour — PASS

**Colour: 8.7 / 10.** The aubergine neutrals carry the magenta's temperature exactly as the spine rule demands, and #D946EF at ~5.7:1 reads as the concentrate of the ground rather than a sticker. It loses a little on long-scroll soothing — magenta at this saturation is a deliberate jolt — but the dose is small enough that it earns the memorability back.

**Design: 8.6 / 10.** The vertical eyebrow rail with the headline growing out of it is a genuinely distinct composition, and accent discipline is clean: dot, button, tag — three roles, headline untouched. The baseline band tying sub and CTA under a hairline is the one slightly conventional move in an otherwise off-axis layout.

## 08 · Acid — FAIL

**Colour: 8.3 / 10.** The olive-shifted neutrals (#0C0D08 / #15170F / #262918) fixed the real problem — the lime now grows out of the ground instead of floating on dead grey, and 15:1 contrast is untouchable. But I scored this hardest last round and the honest flaw stands: #C8F526 vibrates against the near-black, and over a long scroll it is the least soothing and least "stranger-pays-money" palette of the four.

**Design: 8.6 / 10.** The banded structure — indented eyebrow strip, open stage, split foot, services grid below — is the most distinctive layout of my five and typography (heavy uppercase Space Grotesk at .9 line-height) matches the palette's register exactly. Accent sits in three roles (square, button, tag) and never touches the headline.

**What would fix it:** the colour score, specifically the vibration. Pull the accent one notch toward chartreuse-olive — roughly #A8D626 territory — keeping it above 4.5:1 (there is enormous headroom at 15:1), or lift the ground a step lighter so the same lime stops buzzing. Either change buys back the soothing/trust deficit without touching the concept.

## 09 · One Note — PASS (current state, ice)

**Colour: 8.7 / 10.** The cool-tinted neutrals (#0A0B0D / #141619 / #242830 / #98A0AC) lean blue coherently, so #BBD9F0 reads as the page's own atmosphere distilled — the spine rule is satisfied properly, not technically. It sits lighter than the L 60–75% band the spec prefers, which costs it a little boldness, but at 13.47:1 it is crisp rather than washed-out, and on this ground it reads as ice, not pastel.

**Design: 8.6 / 10.** The dead-centred column between two hairlines with one chromatic note used twice (square mark, pill button) is the discipline the concept promises, and the tight Inter tracking matches the restrained register. Two honest dents: centred layouts are the most common move in the ten, and the file's header comment still says "amber" — stale documentation of a decision that no longer exists.

## 10 · Fog — FAIL

**Colour: 8.3 / 10.** The periwinkle #9AA5FF at 8.7:1 is gentle and the high text/muted contrast keeps it legible, but the neutrals (#16161A, #2C2C33, #8E8E98) carry only a two-to-four-point blue lean — the spine is present on paper and nearly invisible in practice. Soothing is its strength; memorability and spine are where it stays short of the bar.

**Design: 8.4 / 10.** The small-scale Newsreader set directly on the ground with the eyebrow anchored to the top edge is quiet in a way none of the others attempt, and the whitespace is genuinely generous rather than empty. But "quiet left column with dot-eyebrow" is the closest any of these comes to a generic portfolio hero, and against nine louder layouts it needs one more structural gesture to be unmistakably its own thing.

**What would fix it:** deepen the blue-violet lean in the neutrals (bg toward #14141C, border toward #2A2A38) so the periwinkle reads as native rather than adjacent, and add one compositional mark — a hairline rule the headline hangs from, or a right-aligned column — so the layout can't be mistaken for the default hero.

## The amber question

I accept it. No argument. #F0B440 was the exact colour family the client rejected at the start of this project — the rejection is the reason ten prototypes exist — and choosing it anyway was a brief violation regardless of how well it scored on contrast. The fact that I wrapped it in warm-tinted neutrals makes it worse, not better: I rebuilt the rejected direction coherently instead of accidentally. The ice replacement is the right call, and 09 scores higher with it than it would have with the amber.
